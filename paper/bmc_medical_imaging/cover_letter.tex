\subsection*{Clinical and Computational Significance for \textit{BMC Medical Imaging}}
In modern high-resolution 3D Computed Tomography (CT) and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), volumetric reconstruction and forward projection require processing massive isotropic grids ($512^3$ to $1024^3$ voxels). To fit within GPU memory and L2 caches, modern clinical reconstruction frameworks partition volumes across distributed GPU nodes, MPI ranks, or tiled cache blocks. 

Prevailing volumetric imaging pipelines decouple continuous ray clipping from discrete voxel stepping: continuous trajectories are clipped against sub-volume bounding boxes, and the resulting boundary intersections are rounded to grid coordinates to reseed standard Bresenham steppers. In this paper, we demonstrate that this decoupled approach resets fractional error accumulators to zero, creating a systematic phase shift that disrupts discrete ray stepping. This defect corrupts over 99.7\% of boundary-crossing rays, causing boundary seam gaps and missing voxels that generate line-integral attenuation errors up to 15.94\,cm$^{-1}$ ($\mathrm{RMSE} = 8.9049$, $\mathrm{PSNR} = 14.99$\,dB on 512 sub-volumes). In full clinical sinogram projections of patient CT and MRI scans, 90.17\% to 92.20\% of all detector bins are corrupted by phase-shift artifacts, impairing the convergence of iterative algebraic reconstruction techniques (SART, SIRT, MLEM).

\subsection*{Key Contributions}
To eliminate these artifacts without communication overhead, this manuscript introduces the \textbf{Analytical Phase-Compensated Bresenham (AP-Bresenham)} algorithm:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Closed-Form Diophantine Dynamics:} We derive the exact analytical equations governing multi-dimensional digital straight lines, establishing closed-form formulations for discrete coordinates and internal accumulator states.
    \item \textbf{Pure-Integer $O(1)$ Boundary Inversion:} We formulate exact $O(1)$ boundary inversion formulas directly in $\mathbb{Z}$, completely avoiding floating-point rounding errors and iterative searches.
    \item \textbf{Analytical Historical Phase Injection:} We analytically restore the exact fractional accumulator state at any entry boundary in $O(1)$ time, guaranteeing 100\% bit-level equivalence to monolithic traversal without requiring inter-node communication or ghost-cell buffers.
    \item \textbf{Clinical Patient Validation:} Evaluated across synthetic 3D Shepp-Logan phantoms, multi-compartment brain MRI models, and real clinical acquisitions (human abdominal CT and MNI-152 brain MRI) partitioned into up to 512 sub-volumes, AP-Bresenham achieves a 0.00\% seam defect rate, zero corrupted voxels, exact Radon line-integral conservation ($\mathrm{RMSE} = 0.0000, \mathrm{PSNR} = \infty$), and a 1.2$\times$--1.6$\times$ setup speedup over floating-point clippers.
\end{enumerate}
