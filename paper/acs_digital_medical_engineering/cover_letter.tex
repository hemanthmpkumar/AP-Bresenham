\subsection*{Scope and Significance for \textit{ACS Digital Medical Engineering}}
High-resolution 3D volumetric medical imaging---including Computed Tomography (CT), Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI), and PET/CT multi-modality scanning---demands spatial grid partitioning across distributed GPU clusters, MPI ranks, or tiled cache blocks to process massive voxel arrays ($512^3$ to $1024^3$ voxels). Existing ray traversal pipelines clip continuous trajectories against sub-volume bounding boxes and round boundary intersections to integer coordinates to reseed standard Bresenham steppers. 

In this work, we reveal that this conventional decoupled clipping strategy fundamentally resets fractional error accumulators, inducing a \emph{Bresenham Phase-Shift Phenomenon} that corrupts voxel traversal paths across over 99.7\% of boundary-crossing rays. In iterative tomographic reconstruction (SART, SIRT, MLEM), this introduces line-integral attenuation errors reaching up to 15.9\,cm$^{-1}$ and produces severe boundary streak and ring artifacts that impair diagnostic precision and mathematical convergence.

\subsection*{Key Advances Relative to the State-of-the-Art}
To address this 60-year-old challenge at the interface of discrete geometry and computational biomedical imaging, this manuscript introduces the \textbf{Analytical Phase-Compensated Bresenham (AP-Bresenham)} algorithm:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Closed-Form Diophantine Dynamics:} We derive the exact analytical equations governing multi-dimensional digital straight lines, establishing the Diophantine conditions of discrete ray stepping.
    \item \textbf{Pure-Integer $O(1)$ Boundary Inversion:} We formulate exact $O(1)$ boundary inversion formulas directly in $\mathbb{Z}$, completely avoiding floating-point rounding errors and iterative searches.
    \item \textbf{Analytical Historical Phase Injection:} We analytically restore the exact fractional accumulator state at any entry boundary in $O(1)$ time, guaranteeing 100\% bit-level equivalence to monolithic traversal without requiring inter-node communication or ghost-cell buffers.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Clinical Patient Validation:} Evaluated across synthetic Shepp-Logan phantoms, multi-compartment brain MRI models, and open clinical acquisitions (human abdominal CT and MNI-152 brain MRI) partitioned into up to 512 sub-volumes, AP-Bresenham completely eliminates seam artifacts, achieves exact Radon line-integral conservation ($\mathrm{RMSE} = 0.0000, \mathrm{PSNR} = \infty$), and delivers a 1.2$\times$--1.6$\times$ setup speedup over floating-point clippers.
\end{enumerate}
